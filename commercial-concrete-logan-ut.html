Commercial Concrete Contractors in Logan & Cache Valley

Commercial concrete has to do more than look good — it has to carry heavy loads, meet code and ADA requirements, and hold up to constant traffic and weather. Cache County Concrete helps business owners, property managers and general contractors throughout Logan, North Logan, Smithfield, Providence, Hyrum, Nibley and Cache Valley find licensed local contractors who handle commercial flatwork and structural concrete.

Commercial Services Offered

  • Parking lots & drive lanes — heavy-duty slabs built for traffic loads.
  • Sidewalks & walkways — including ADA-compliant routes.
  • ADA ramps & truncated domes — built to accessibility standards.
  • Approaches, aprons & curb and gutter.
  • Loading docks & dumpster pads — reinforced for heavy, repeated loads.
  • Equipment & mechanical pads.
  • Foundations, footings & warehouse/shop slabs.

What Sets Commercial Concrete Apart

  • Load ratings & thickness — slabs engineered for the vehicles and equipment they’ll carry.
  • Reinforcement — heavier rebar and proper jointing for high-traffic durability.
  • ADA compliance — ramps, slopes and detectable warnings built to spec.
  • Scheduling — work is phased to keep your business open and minimize disruption.
  • Drainage & grading — engineered so water moves where it should.

What Does Commercial Concrete Cost?

Commercial pricing depends entirely on scope and specs — square footage, slab thickness and load rating, reinforcement, site prep, drainage and ADA requirements all factor in. For general contractors and property managers, the licensed contractor you’re referred to can provide detailed bids you can build into your project budget. For business owners, they provide a free, clear quote. Either way, the right number comes from reviewing the plans or walking the site.

The Commercial Process

  1. Site evaluation & bid — the contractor reviews plans or walks the site and provides a detailed bid.
  2. Prep & excavation — demolition, grading and a compacted base built to the load spec.
  3. Forming & reinforcement — forms set to grade with rebar sized for commercial loads.
  4. Pour & finish — the correct mix poured, finished and jointed for durability.
  5. Cure & clean-up — proper curing and a clean, ready-to-use site.

Commercial Concrete FAQ

Do these contractors bid general contractor projects?

Yes. Detailed, itemized bids are provided for GCs and property managers, coordinated with your schedule, specs and inspections.

Is ADA-compliant work handled?

Yes — ADA ramps, slopes, landings and truncated-dome detectable warnings built to current accessibility standards.

Can work be done after hours or in phases to avoid disrupting my business?

Commercial work is routinely phased and can be scheduled around your operating hours so customers and staff keep access while the pour happens section by section.

How thick is a commercial parking lot slab?

Thicker and more heavily reinforced than residential — the exact thickness depends on the expected traffic and loads, which are spec’d to the project.

Is commercial tear-out and replacement handled?

Yes. Demolition, haul-away and repour of failed commercial flatwork — including parking lots, sidewalks and pads — are all part of the service.

Are permits pulled and inspections met?

Contractors build to code and coordinate with the required inspections. Permitting is typically handled by the owner or GC, and the contractor works within it to keep the project moving.

Minimizing Downtime for Your Business

For a business, concrete work isn’t just about the slab — it’s about staying open while it gets done. A good commercial contractor plans projects around your operations: pouring in phases so part of a parking lot or sidewalk stays usable, scheduling after-hours or weekend work where it makes sense, and sequencing the job so customers and deliveries can still get in and out.

They also handle the details that keep a commercial project compliant and durable — proper slab thickness and reinforcement for heavy loads and traffic, ADA-compliant ramps and sidewalk slopes, and clean transitions to existing pavement. Share your hours and constraints at the bid stage and the contractor will build the schedule around them.
